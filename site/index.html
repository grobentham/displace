      Trade with a clear head.

      Most blown accounts aren't caused by a bad model. They're caused by a tired, angry or scared trader breaking a good one. This page looks after the person behind the chart.

      Your contract with yourself

      My rules

      Write up to five rules in your own words, then save the card as an image for your phone wallpaper or desk.

      Ten traps, and a circuit breaker for each.

      01

      FOMO

      Price is running without you, so you chase a late entry far from your level.

      Circuit breakerIf it isn't on your checklist, it isn't your trade. Write down the setup you missed and review it later.

      02

      Revenge trading

      You just lost and want it back now, so you re-enter bigger and faster.

      Circuit breakerAfter any loss, stand up and leave the screen for 15 minutes. Never raise size on the same day as a loss.

      03

      Tilt

      Frustration takes over. You click without a plan and stop caring about the rules.

      Circuit breakerSet a hard stop: two losses or your daily loss limit, whichever comes first, and the platform closes.

      04

      Loss aversion

      You move the stop "just a little" because taking the loss feels worse than the risk.

      Circuit breakerSize the trade so the full stop-out is boring. If a loss would hurt, the position is too big.

      05

      Overconfidence

      After a winning streak you feel untouchable, so you skip steps and add size.

      Circuit breakerChange size only on a schedule you set in advance, such as after 20 logged trades, never mid-streak.

      06

      Outcome bias

      You judge a trade by whether it made money, not by whether you followed the plan.

      Circuit breakerGrade every trade A to C on process before you look at the P&L. A rule-breaking winner is a C.

      07

      Boredom trading

      Nothing is setting up, so you take something just to be in a trade.

      Circuit breakerNo setup by the end of your session window means no trade. A flat day is a rule followed, not a day wasted.

      08

      Analysis paralysis

      Ten timeframes, six indicators, and you still can't decide, so you miss it or enter late.

      Circuit breakerOne model, one timeframe pair, one checklist. If every box is ticked, the decision is already made.

      09

      Fear of pulling the trigger

      After a few losses a clean setup appears and you freeze, then watch it run.

      Circuit breakerDrop to the smallest size you can trade until you've taken five setups in a row by the book.

      10

      Comparison

      Someone's profit screenshot makes your steady week feel like failure, so you reach for more risk.

      Circuit breakerYou see their best day, never their account history. Mute the feed during your session and compare only with your own last month.

      After a blown account or a bad week

      The recovery plan

      The days after a big loss are when the worst decisions get made. Follow these in order, and don't skip ahead.

      1. StopClose the platform today. No "one last trade" to get it back.
      2. Step away for three daysNo charts, no trading feeds. Sleep, eat properly, see people.
      3. Review with a cool headGo through the trades and name what broke: the model, the size, or the rules.
      4. Return on demoTen trades by the book on a demo or the replay drills before any real money.
      5. Come back at half sizeStay at half risk until you've logged twenty trades that followed the plan.
      Prop firm pressure

      Evaluations play with your head

      The deadline
      A time limit makes you force trades. Divide the target by the days you have and aim for that, not for a hero day.
      "One trade from passing"
      That is when people double their size and fail. The last trade is sized exactly like the first.
      The daily loss limit
      Set your own limit at half of the firm's. Hitting yours means you still have an account tomorrow.
      A failed evaluation
      It's a fee, not a verdict on you. Take a week before you buy another one.

      Look after the trader, not just the account.

      Signs trading is hurting you

      • You trade money you need for rent, bills or debt.
      • You hide losses or your trading from people close to you.
      • You can't stop after hitting your loss limit.
      • You're losing sleep, skipping meals or checking charts all night.
      • Your mood for the whole day depends on your P&L.
      • You keep depositing to win back what you lost.

      Habits that protect you

      • Only risk money you can afford to lose completely.
      • Fixed hours: one session, then the charts close.
      • Sleep, food and movement come before the open.
      • One person who knows your real results.
      • Planned days off, and a week off after a big drawdown.
      • A life outside trading that doesn't depend on today's result.

      If several of those warning signs sound like you, stop trading for now and talk to someone you trust. That's not weakness. It's risk management.

        Know the releases

        What each report measures, why markets follow it, and which figures to look for.

        How to read a release

        Actual vs forecast

        The actual is the figure published by the agency. When a consensus forecast is available, the difference is the surprise.

        Revisions

        Agencies may revise earlier figures as more information becomes available. A release can contain both a new reading and revisions to previous periods.

        Why the surprise matters

        Markets price the consensus in advance. What moves them is the gap between the actual and what was expected, not whether the number is high or low.

        The first minutes

        Figures land at the exact minute shown. Spreads widen and price can whip both ways first, so many traders wait for the first 15 minutes to settle.

        Method

        How Displace tests.

        Research compares results against baselines and documents the assumptions behind each test. Displace Open tracks eligibility and outcomes for published forecasts.

        Base rates first

        NQ closes higher on about 54% of days and gold on about 53%. A signal has to beat those numbers, not 50%, because "always long" already gets that far.

        Walk-forward testing

        Models are refit every year using only the years before it, then scored on that year. Nothing is judged on the data it was fitted to.

        Proper scoring

        Probabilities are scored with the Brier score against the base rate. Hit rates alone reward overconfidence; Brier skill doesn't.

        Significance and stability

        A signal must be significant after accounting for every signal tried across all eight markets (a Diebold–Mariano test with a Bonferroni split, about t ≥ 3.2), must hold in both halves of the history and the last three years, and for the stock indices must also show up in most of the other indices.

        Shown, not hidden

        Signals that fail stay on the board with their scores, so you can see what was tried. Most daily direction signals fail; that's the normal result for liquid futures.

        Verified forecast scoring

        A forecast needs a deployment receipt before the target session starts and a valid outcome to enter the verified scorecard.

        What gets forecast

        The outlook focuses on estimating the session range and the chance of reaching price levels. The direction figure shows the historical up-day frequency.

        Volatility (HAR)

        The next day's range from yesterday's, last week's and last month's ranges, implied volatility, release days and Mondays. Refit every year; scored against a plain 20-day average.

        Touch ladder

        How often price reached the close plus or minus 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 forecast standard deviations, measured only with forecasts made before each day.

        Highs and lows

        The chance of trading through today's high or low, from how far the close sits from each. Shown with its calibration: when it says 70%, how often that happened.

        Sessions

        Overnight against New York hours, and whether the first hour predicts the last, from hourly futures bars that are archived every run.

        Cross-asset inputs

        Implied volatility indices, the VIX term structure, credit spreads, real yields, the dollar and the 2-year yield, used one day late because that is when FRED publishes them.

        Position size

        Contracts for a given dollar risk with a stop at a multiple of the forecast standard deviation, so size shrinks automatically when the market is wild.

        Terms

        Base rate
        How often something happens on all days. The bar every signal has to clear.
        Brier score
        The mean squared error of a probability. Lower is better; a forecast that just says the base rate sets the benchmark.
        Brier skill
        The improvement over the base-rate forecast, in percent. Above zero means the signal adds something.
        Walk-forward
        Fit on the past, score on the next period, roll forward. The closest a backtest gets to live trading.
        Out of sample
        Data the model never saw while it was being fitted.
        z / t statistic
        How many standard errors a result sits from "no effect". About 2 is the usual bar for one test; higher when many are tried.
        Average range
        The mean high-to-low range over the last 20 days. Tomorrow's range tracks it closely, which is why ranges can be forecast and direction mostly can't.
        Surprise
        The actual figure minus the consensus forecast, scaled by how big surprises usually are for that report.

        Questions? Answers.

        Is Displace free?

        Yes. Every page and tool is free, and none of it needs an account. Tools and settings save in your browser.

        Do you sell signals or trade calls?

        No. Displace publishes statistics: probabilities, ranges and historical reactions, each tested against a base rate with its live record in public. It never gives entries, stops, targets or position sizes, and nothing here guarantees returns.

        Why is the daily bias usually just the base rate?

        Because that's what the evidence says. Next-day direction for liquid index and gold futures is very hard to predict, and most published signals fail out of sample. Displace only moves the probability for signals that pass its tests, and shows the ones that don't. Ranges and levels are much more predictable, so that's where the useful numbers are.

        Where does the data come from?

        Free public sources: Yahoo Finance futures prices, the BLS, BEA, Census, Federal Reserve, Treasury and FRED, with consensus forecasts from the TradingView and ForexFactory calendars. The Method page lists them.

        Which markets does it cover?

        NQ (Nasdaq-100) and gold futures for the bias and release reactions, plus the dollar and the 2-year yield for release studies. The risk tools work for any instrument.

        Why is it called Displace?

        A large move away from where price was is where risk and opportunity both sit. Displace measures how often, how far and how reliably, instead of guessing.
