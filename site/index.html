    Stage 1 of 6Foundations

    Structure. Liquidity. Value.

    The three building blocks every other concept stands on.

    Market structure

    Know when the trend turns.

    Read structure from swing points. Higher highs and higher lows are bullish; lower highs and lower lows are bearish.

    • BOS: a close beyond a swing in the trend's direction.
    • MSS: the swing that started the last counter-move breaks, with displacement.
    • Displacement: a strong move that leaves a fair value gap.
    Liquidity

    Price hunts the stops.

    Above every obvious high sits buyside liquidity. Below every obvious low sits sellside liquidity. Large orders need that flow to fill.

    • Equal highs and lows stack stops in one place.
    • Price alternates between external range liquidity and the internal gaps.
    • Close back inside: a raid. Close beyond with force: a run.
    Premium, discount, OTE

    Buy cheap. Sell expensive.

    Measure a swing. Its 50% is equilibrium. Buy only in discount, sell only in premium.

    • Optimal trade entry: the 62–79% retracement, 70.5% sweet spot.
    • Targets: the leg extreme, then −0.27 and −0.62.

    Price moves from liquidity to liquidity. Everything else is detail.

    Checkpoint · Stage 1 complete

    You can read structure and spot liquidity. That's most of the edge.

    Power of 3

    Open. Fake. Expand.

    Every candle has three phases: accumulation, manipulation (the Judas swing), and distribution. On a bullish day, buy below the midnight open.

    Standard deviations

    Project the day.

    A quiet pre-session range projects the day's run in multiples of its own height (a "standard deviation" here, not a statistical one). Use the CBDR (14:00–20:00), ideally 40 pips or less in FX, or the Asian range (20:00–00:00) when the CBDR is too wide.

    Stage 4 of 6Models

    Nineteen ways to execute.

    Start with a bias. Then pick a model that combines a raid, an array and a window in a fixed order.

    01

    Find the draw

    Old high with equal highs above, or an unfilled daily gap below? Check the 20/40/60-day ranges.

    02

    Read the candles

    Did yesterday close above the prior high, or reject from it?

    03

    Find the range

    On the 4H or 1H, is price in premium or discount?

    04

    Confirm in session

    The opposite side gets taken first, then structure shifts in a kill zone.

    How to study a model
    1. Read the idea. Know why it works before you learn the steps.
    2. Walk the chart. Step through it until you can say what comes next.
    3. Learn when to skip it. Most losses come from forcing a setup.
    4. Practise, then test. Use the replay drills and checklist, and log 20 examples before trading it live.
    Market maker models

    Down the curve, and back.

    Price leaves an original consolidation, steps down, reverses at a higher-timeframe array, and climbs back to where it started.

    SMT divergence

    One sweeps. One holds.

    When ES makes a lower low and NQ doesn't, the sell-off isn't supported. Confirm with ES · NQ · YM, EURUSD · GBPUSD, or DXY inverse.

    Anatomy of a trade

    One morning. Six decisions.

    An illustrative NQ session traded with the 2022 Mentorship model, from pre-market plan to profit.

    1. Mark the drawPre-market: obvious buyside liquidity sits above the session's earlier high. That's the target.
    2. Find the baitLondon left a clean low. Sellside liquidity is resting just under it.
    3. Watch the raidInside the NY AM kill zone, price dips through the low and takes those stops.
    4. Confirm the shiftA displacement candle closes above the last lower high: MSS, leaving a fair value gap.
    5. Enter at the gapBuy the retrace into the FVG. Stop goes under the raid low.
    6. Get paid at liquidityPrice runs into the buyside target. About 1.5R on this example.

    Stage 6 of 6Prove it

    Test it honestly.

    ICT is discretionary and mostly taught through hindsight markups. Before trading real size, prove it works for you: test it, manage risk, and journal every trade.

    01

    Define it

    Turn every concept into a rule a script can check: swing size, minimum gap, displacement.

    02

    Backtest

    Code it in Pine or Python. Test on data you didn't look at while designing it.

    03

    Isolate

    Kill zones are the busiest hours. Compare each model to random entries in the same window.

    04

    Journal

    Log forward trades. Track hit rate, average R and drawdown per model.

    Playbook

    A good read deserves a good trade.

    %

    Size from the stop.

    • Small fixed risk, often 1% or less.
    • Halve your risk after a loss, until equity recovers.
    • Daily loss limit.
    ⌖

    Stops by structure.

    • Beyond the raid swing.
    • Or past the FVG's candle.
    • Never inside an open gap.
    ↗

    Pay at liquidity.

    • Partial at internal liquidity.
    • Runner to the old high/low.
    • Break-even after a new BOS.
    1

    One good trade.

    • One model, one kill zone.
    • Skip unplanned news.
    • Stop at your daily target.

    Common mistakes, fixed.

    Avoid

    • Marking every gap until the chart is a wall of boxes.
    • Buying a bullish array that sits in premium.
    • Calling a wick an MSS.
    • Trading outside any kill zone.
    • Redrawing levels after the fact.

    Instead

    • Mark only arrays between price and the draw.
    • Check premium or discount every time.
    • Require a body close and a gap left behind.
    • Trade one window you can focus on.
    • Screenshot before entry, then journal.

    Your contract with yourself

    My rules

    Write up to five rules in your own words, then save the card as an image for your phone wallpaper or desk.

    Ten traps, and a circuit breaker for each.

    01

    FOMO

    Price is running without you, so you chase a late entry far from your level.

    Circuit breakerIf it isn't on your checklist, it isn't your trade. Write down the setup you missed and review it later.

    02

    Revenge trading

    You just lost and want it back now, so you re-enter bigger and faster.

    Circuit breakerAfter any loss, stand up and leave the screen for 15 minutes. Never raise size on the same day as a loss.

    03

    Tilt

    Frustration takes over. You click without a plan and stop caring about the rules.

    Circuit breakerSet a hard stop: two losses or your daily loss limit, whichever comes first, and the platform closes.

    04

    Loss aversion

    You move the stop "just a little" because taking the loss feels worse than the risk.

    Circuit breakerSize the trade so the full stop-out is boring. If a loss would hurt, the position is too big.

    05

    Overconfidence

    After a winning streak you feel untouchable, so you skip steps and add size.

    Circuit breakerChange size only on a schedule you set in advance, such as after 20 logged trades, never mid-streak.

    06

    Outcome bias

    You judge a trade by whether it made money, not by whether you followed the plan.

    Circuit breakerGrade every trade A to C on process before you look at the P&L. A rule-breaking winner is a C.

    07

    Boredom trading

    Nothing is setting up, so you take something just to be in a trade.

    Circuit breakerNo setup by the end of your kill zone means no trade. A flat day is a rule followed, not a day wasted.

    08

    Analysis paralysis

    Ten timeframes, six indicators, and you still can't decide, so you miss it or enter late.

    Circuit breakerOne model, one timeframe pair, one checklist. If every box is ticked, the decision is already made.

    09

    Fear of pulling the trigger

    After a few losses a clean setup appears and you freeze, then watch it run.

    Circuit breakerDrop to the smallest size you can trade until you've taken five setups in a row by the book.

    10

    Comparison

    Someone's profit screenshot makes your steady week feel like failure, so you reach for more risk.

    Circuit breakerYou see their best day, never their account history. Mute the feed during your session and compare only with your own last month.

    After a blown account or a bad week

    The recovery plan

    The days after a big loss are when the worst decisions get made. Follow these in order, and don't skip ahead.

    1. StopClose the platform today. No "one last trade" to get it back.
    2. Step away for three daysNo charts, no trading feeds. Sleep, eat properly, see people.
    3. Review with a cool headGo through the trades and name what broke: the model, the size, or the rules.
    4. Return on demoTen trades by the book on a demo or the replay drills before any real money.
    5. Come back at half sizeStay at half risk until you've logged twenty trades that followed the plan.
    Prop firm pressure

    Evaluations play with your head

    The deadline
    A time limit makes you force trades. Divide the target by the days you have and aim for that, not for a hero day.
    "One trade from passing"
    That is when people double their size and fail. The last trade is sized exactly like the first.
    The daily loss limit
    Set your own limit at half of the firm's. Hitting yours means you still have an account tomorrow.
    A failed evaluation
    It's a fee, not a verdict on you. Take a week before you buy another one.

    Look after the trader, not just the account.

    Signs trading is hurting you

    • You trade money you need for rent, bills or debt.
    • You hide losses or your trading from people close to you.
    • You can't stop after hitting your loss limit.
    • You're losing sleep, skipping meals or checking charts all night.
    • Your mood for the whole day depends on your P&L.
    • You keep depositing to win back what you lost.

    Habits that protect you

    • Only risk money you can afford to lose completely.
    • Fixed hours: one session, then the charts close.
    • Sleep, food and movement come before the open.
    • One person who knows your real results.
    • Planned days off, and a week off after a big drawdown.
    • A life outside trading that doesn't depend on today's result.

    If several of those warning signs sound like you, stop trading for now and talk to someone you trust. That's not weakness. It's risk management.

      How to read a release

      Actual vs forecast

      The actual is the figure published by the agency. When a consensus forecast is available, the difference is the surprise.

      Revisions

      Agencies may revise earlier figures as more information becomes available. A release can contain both a new reading and revisions to previous periods.
